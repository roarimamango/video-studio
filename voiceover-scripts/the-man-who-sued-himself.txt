Gerald Pibb sued Gerald Pibb. And no matter what the judge decided, Gerald Pibb was going to lose.

It started on a Tuesday. Gerald stepped out of his front door, onto his own icy driveway, and slipped. He broke his wrist, his phone, and his dignity, in that order.

So he called his insurance company. And a very polite woman explained that his policy only covered injuries caused by the homeowner's negligence, to someone else. Gerald was not someone else. Gerald was the homeowner.

Gerald thought about this for a long time. Then he had an idea.

What if the homeowner, Gerald, had been negligent? And what if the victim, also Gerald, sued him?

His lawyer told him it was the stupidest thing he'd ever heard. Then he told him the fee. Gerald couldn't afford one lawyer, let alone two. So he did the only reasonable thing. He represented both sides.

On day one, Gerald stood up and said, "Your Honor, my client was carelessly injured on a driveway that the defendant never salted."

Then he walked to the other table, sat down, and said, "Objection. The defendant was busy that morning."

The judge asked, "Busy doing what?"

And both Geralds said, at the same time, "Slipping."

The trial lasted three weeks. Gerald cross-examined himself and caught himself in a lie. He asked for a short recess to calm himself down. At one point, he tried to settle out of court, and he refused.

Finally, the jury reached a verdict. They found the homeowner guilty of negligence, and awarded the victim forty thousand dollars.

Gerald the victim jumped up and cheered. Gerald the defendant put his head in his hands. Both of them hugged.

The insurance company had no choice. They sent the victim a check for forty thousand dollars.

And then, the next morning, they sent the homeowner a letter. Because of his proven history of dangerous negligence, his premium was going up. By exactly forty thousand dollars.

Gerald had spent three weeks, broken one wrist, and won and lost the same case, only to end up exactly where he started.

He was furious. He called his lawyer. His lawyer said, "Who are you going to sue now? Yourself?"

Gerald said, "Don't be ridiculous."

And then he stepped outside, slipped on the same patch of ice, and broke his other wrist.

He's suing the ice.
