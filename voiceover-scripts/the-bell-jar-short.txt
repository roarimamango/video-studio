The Bell Jar is a novel. But almost everything in it really happened.

In 1953, Sylvia Plath was twenty, a star student, and a guest editor at Mademoiselle magazine in New York. On her last night there, she climbed to the roof of her hotel and threw her clothes into the night.

Back home, she sank into a deep depression. That August, she went missing for three days, and was found barely alive. She had tried to end her life.

Eight years later, she turned that summer into a novel. Every character was a real person in disguise, so she published it under a fake name: Victoria Lucas.

It came out in January 1963. A month later, Sylvia Plath died. She was thirty.

Her mother fought to keep the book out of America. She lost. Today, it's one of the most famous novels ever written about depression.
