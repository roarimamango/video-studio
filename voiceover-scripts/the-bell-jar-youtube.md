Almost everything in Sylvia Plath's The Bell Jar really happened. The hotel, the hospital, the doctors, the boyfriend, the girl herself. And the woman who wrote it published it under a fake name, weeks before her death.

This is the lore behind The Bell Jar: Plath's summer at Mademoiselle magazine in 1953, the breakdown that followed, the real people hidden behind every character, the secret pseudonym "Victoria Lucas", the meaning of the bell jar and the famous fig tree, and how a book her own mother fought to suppress became one of the most important novels ever written about depression.
