# The Bell Jar — 3 Shorts scripts (~45–55 s each)
Each one ends by pointing to the full video. Link the full video to each Short in YouTube Studio ("Related video").

## Short 1 — The fig tree
There's a passage in a 1963 novel that millions of people share online. A young woman sees her life as a fig tree. Every fig is a different future. A famous poet. A professor. An editor. A wife and mother. A life traveling the world. She wants all of them. But choosing one means losing the rest. And as she sits there, unable to decide, the figs begin to wrinkle and go black, and drop, one by one, to the ground at her feet. The book is The Bell Jar. And the woman who wrote it was describing her own life. The full story is on my channel.

## Short 2 — The fake name
In January 1963, a novel came out in London by an author nobody had heard of: Victoria Lucas. Victoria Lucas didn't exist. The real author was a thirty-year-old American poet named Sylvia Plath. She used a fake name because almost every character in the book was a real person: her mother, her old boyfriend, her doctors, the woman who paid for her hospital care. Less than a month later, Sylvia Plath died by suicide. Her mother fought for years to keep the book out of America. She lost. Today, The Bell Jar has never gone out of print. ## Short 3 — The roof of the Barbizon
In June 1953, Sylvia Plath was living the dream. Twenty years old, a straight-A student, and one of twenty young women picked to work at Mademoiselle, one of America's most glamorous magazines. She stayed at the Barbizon, a famous hotel for women where men weren't allowed past the lobby. But she felt nothing. On her last night in New York, she climbed onto the hotel roof and threw her clothes, piece by piece, into the night air. Eight years later, she turned that summer into a novel: The Bell Jar.
